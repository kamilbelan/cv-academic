\section{Theses}
\cventry{2026}{Numerical methods for the simulation of air flow past a mountain}{master's thesis}{supervisor Assoc. Prof. Michal Pavelka}{}{The objective of the thesis is to further develop and verify the SPH method for compressible atmospheric flow over orography. The work relies on a reformulation of the problem in the potential temperature variable, robust terrain-following orography description and improved treatment of inflow and outflow boundary conditions. The numerical instability of the hydrostatic balance is addressed by the development of novel well-balanced schemes and adaptive techniques. }
\cvitem{Codebase}{(both theses) \url{https://github.com/kamilbelan/sph-mountain-waves}}
\cventry{2024}{Simulating flows past a mountain range using smoothed particle hydrodynamics}{bachelor's thesis}{supervisor Assoc. Prof. Michal Pavelka}{}{The main objective of the thesis is to use the Lagrangian numerical method SPH to simulate compressible atmospheric flow over orography, a problem typically solved by mesh-based methods (FVM, FDM). It was shown by numerical evidence that the simulation of hydrostatic balance of a stratified atmosphere by the standard SPH method is so unstable that SPH in its standard formulation is completely unable to capture phenomena such as internal gravity waves.}
